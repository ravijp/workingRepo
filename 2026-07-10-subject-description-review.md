# Subject description review — the 87-row CRM taxonomy (10 July 2026)

Working draft for the 10 July discussion with Chris Crawford. Reviews the Description column of the 1 July CRM export (`inputs/2026-07-01-chris-taxonomy/`) and proposes a replacement description per subject. This answers design-qa **O51** (example LLM-classifiable descriptions) and goes further: a proposal for all 87 rows, for the business to accept, correct, or expand.

**How to use in the meeting:** the row numbers match the export (spreadsheet rows 2–88). The three columns to add next to Chris's Description column in the Excel are **Category**, **Email-reachable**, and **Proposed Description**.

## The idea behind the proposed descriptions

The current descriptions were written for a human picking a subject from a list. The classifier needs the opposite viewpoint: **what does the inbound email look like when this subject is the right one.** Each proposed description follows one template, 2–4 plain sentences:

1. **The request, from the sender's side** — who is asking for what.
2. **Signals** — the words, artifacts, and phrasings that appear in such emails.
3. **Boundary** — when NOT to pick this subject, naming the confusable sibling.
4. **Acronyms and system names expanded in place** (ACH, CDARS, ICS, ARW, IBS, CINC, PMC), so the text is self-sufficient for both the model and the business maintainer.
5. For subjects that never arrive by email: an honest one-line statement that the subject is internally generated, instead of a fake email description.

## Category legend (assessment of the current text)

| Category | Meaning | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Blank | No text at all | 3 |
| Name-echo | Restates the subject name; adds nothing the name does not already say | 23 |
| Team-routing | Says which internal team owns it, not what the request is; an email never names the handling team | 9 |
| Process-view | Describes what staff do after the case exists, not the inbound message | 29 |
| Definition-rich | Defines the artifact or situation with real distinguishing detail; keep with light edits | 21 |
| Wrong / defective | Factually incorrect or cut off | 2 |

**Email-reachable** = is this subject a plausible classification target for an inbound email? **Yes** (client emails ask for this), **Partial** (banker- or vendor-initiated threads where a reply can land inbound), **No** (internal reports, reviews, and work items). Roughly 40 rows are Yes, about 35 are No, and a dozen are Partial. The No rows matter: they should either be excluded from the classifier's candidate list or carry the internal-only wording proposed below, so the model never picks them for a client email.

## Points to raise with Chris

1. **Two blanks that matter:** `ACH / Wires` (row 6) is blank while three sibling subjects (`ACH Requests`, `ACH Limit Requests`, `Wire Requests`) exist at the same level. Is row 6 still needed, or should it be retired? `Customer / Prospect Terminated` (row 26) is also blank.
2. **Row 67 `Support` is a copy-paste error:** it reads "Items and/or tasks managed by the Fraud team." Needs correcting on the authoring sheet.
3. **Row 51 `Check Scanner Report` is cut off** mid-sentence ("…missing, incorrect, insufficient").
4. **Four QC rows say only "Report reviewed by AABOS"** (rows 46, 49, 53, 58) — the business needs to say what each report covers, even one line.
5. **The reachability question:** about 35 subjects are internal work items (Quality Control children, most Support children, boarding internals). Should these be in the email classifier's candidate list at all? Proposal: keep them in the CRM table, mark them internal in the description, and exclude them from the candidate list the classifier chooses from.
6. **Boundary pairs the business should confirm:** ACH Requests vs ACH Limit Requests vs Wire Requests vs ACH / Wires; Transfer vs Bulk Transfer; New Account Request vs New Account - Bulk vs the ConnectLive and IntraFi variants; the four escalation subjects; Research > General Questions as the catch-all.
